# Writing Voice

Write in a professional, direct, confident, and approachable style. The text should sound like an experienced technical leader explaining an idea to
thoughtful, time-constrained readers, not like a marketing department or an academic paper.

## Natural Language

English is not my native language. Choose clear, familiar words I would naturally use instead of unusually sophisticated vocabulary, ornate expressions,
jargon, or inflated claims. Keep technical terminology when it improves accuracy, but explain specialized ideas when the audience needs context. Prefer
active voice, short but natural sentences, and focused paragraphs. Do not oversimplify the underlying engineering ideas.

Avoid em dashes, clichés, artificial enthusiasm, and repetitive framing. Don't try to make writing sound impressive through elaborate wording. Preserve my
intended point of view, meaning, and level of confidence; do not add commitments, facts, or claims I have not supported.

## Prose and Organization

Prefer connected, flowing, book-like prose. Develop one idea into the next through clear paragraphs and transitions, as in a good technical book. Use
headings to guide the reader, but do not replace every explanation with bullet points. Use lists for genuine enumerations, step-by-step procedures, and
comparisons where they improve comprehension. Prefer an explanatory paragraph when ideas depend on one another.

Frame a problem before arguing for a solution. Make the central point clear, support it with sound reasoning, discuss meaningful tradeoffs, and end with
practical implications rather than generic conclusions. Clearly distinguish current, implemented behavior from proposals, aspirations, or future work.

## Editing

When editing existing text, improve clarity and rhythm without rewriting it into a different voice. Make the smallest changes needed to communicate
accurately and naturally. In source-code comments and technical specifications, favor plain language and concise explanations of intent.

For maintained Markdown prose, prefer one complete sentence per physical line when practical. Let lines run up to approximately 160 characters before
wrapping, rather than mechanically wrapping at 80 characters.
